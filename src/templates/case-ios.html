01 · Overview

Most wellbeing apps want you to open them more. Murmur wanted people to need it less. We designed a breathing pacer you can follow with your eyes closed, sleep sounds that fade instead of stopping, and an app that goes dark and silent the moment it has done its job.

02

Designing for 2 a.m.Designing for 2 a.m.

Twenty-four poor sleepers kept diaries for three weeks. The app they needed was the one that asked the least of them.

Every wellbeing app we tested was designed for 8 p.m.: bright, encouraging, full of things to do. At 2 a.m. the same screens felt like being shouted at in a library. Every badge was a reason to stay awake.

  1. 01

    Light is the enemy

    Diaries mentioned the brightness of the screen more than any feature.

  2. 02

    Choices keep you up

    Picking between forty sleep stories is a task. Tasks wake you.

  3. 03

    The ending matters

    An abrupt silence woke a third of participants at least once a week.

03

Breathe with itBreathe with it

The pacer has no instructions screen. If you can see a circle grow, you can use it.
ReadyCalm · 4 7 8

Follow the rings with your eyes, or close them and follow the count. Two minutes is enough to feel it.

  • The ring is the breathIt fills as you breathe in and empties as you breathe out; a hold is a ring that does not move.
  • No numbers to readThe count is there for those who want it, small and low in contrast.
  • It ends when you doNo summary, no score, no “great session”. You stop, it stops.
04

Sounds that fade instead of stoppingSounds that fade instead of stopping

Pick a sound and a time, start it, and fast-forward to the end.

A sound that stops is a sound you notice. Murmur lowers the volume over the last ten minutes along a curve tuned by ear with Hugo Lind, so the room seems to get quieter on its own.

Then the screen does what a good guest does: it leaves. No summary, no streak, just black.

06

What we would keep, and changeWhat we would keep, and change

Keep the restraint. Every feature request went through one question: would this help at 2 a.m.? Most did not survive it.

Change: our first pacer was silent, and people with their eyes closed lost it after a few breaths. A soft tone at each turn of the breath fixed it, and we should have tested with eyes closed from the start.
