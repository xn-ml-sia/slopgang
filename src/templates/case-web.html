01 · Overview

Lumen’s analysts watch coastlines, dunes and farmland change from orbit. Their tools treated every pass of the satellite as a separate file. Atlas puts twelve years of passes on a single timeline you can scrub like video, lets anomalies surface themselves, and turns what you found into a brief in one step.

02

From files to a timelineFrom files to a timeline

Analysts had 1,733 passes of the satellite over one stretch of coast, and opened them one file at a time.

Every question an analyst asked (when did this dune move, how fast, was it the storm) meant opening dozens of files, lining them up by hand and squinting. The data was continuous; the tool chopped it into pieces.

So the central decision was the simplest one: treat twelve years of imagery as one long piece of footage, and give it the controls people already know from video.

04

Anatomy of the workspaceAnatomy of the workspace

Three parts, in the order analysts use them.
  1. 01 / 03

    The timeline is the spine

    Every pass as one bar, its height the quality of the image. The playhead sets the time for every layer at once.
  2. 02 / 03

    Anomalies surface themselves

    Atlas compares every pass with the season before and lists sudden change, so storms arrive in a list instead of a search.
  3. 03 / 03

    A brief in one step

    Export turns what is on screen (site, layers, time span, flagged events) into a PDF brief with the measurements attached.
05

Designed for large roomsDesigned for large rooms

Atlas runs on analysts’ desks and on the four-metre wall in Lumen’s situation room.

On the wall, the same layout scales by whole steps of the 8 px grid, never by fractions, so lines stay one device pixel wide and type stays crisp from the back of the room.

Every layer is distinguished by pattern as well as tone (contour, stipple, halftone), so nothing depends on colour, and printed briefs read the same in black and white.

06

What we would keep, and changeWhat we would keep, and change

Keep: borrowing the video editor. Analysts learned the timeline in minutes because their hands already knew J, K and L.

Change: we designed anomaly detection as a list, and analysts wanted it as a sound. In the situation room nobody watches the list; a single soft tone when something changes would have been better. It shipped in version 2.
